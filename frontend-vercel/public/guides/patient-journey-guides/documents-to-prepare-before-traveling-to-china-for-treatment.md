## Key Takeaways

- Ask the receiving department for its upload list before translating everything. Different decisions require different records.
- Put the passport name, date of birth and one patient identifier on the index; then verify those fields inside reports, images and pathology.
- Lead with a one-page clinical brief, but preserve signed source reports, complete DICOM studies and pathology material behind it.
- Reconcile medicines by generic name and state allergies as reactions, not merely as a list of substances.
- Send a manifest, test the files and record the hospital's receipt. An upload link or courier tracking number does not prove that clinicians can use the packet.

## Content

A folder can contain 600 pages and still fail at the first clinical question. The wrong scan may be attached, the CT may consist of screenshots, a pathology translation may omit uncertainty, or the passport spelling may not match the hospital account.

Treat document preparation as a **reconstruction test**. A clinician who has never met the patient should be able to answer five questions: Who is this? What happened, in what order? What evidence supports the current diagnosis? What treatment has already been given? What decision is requested now?

### Start with the decision, not the scanner

Write one sentence describing the requested decision: pathology review, surgical candidacy, treatment-line choice, rehabilitation admission, fertility assessment or another defined task. Then ask the intended Chinese department which items are mandatory, preferred and obtainable after arrival.

Do not let a coordinator turn a preliminary document screen into a final diagnosis or acceptance. Record whether the response means only “files received,” “clinically reviewed,” “appropriate for an appointment,” or “formally accepted for admission.”

### Build six separate bundles

#### 1. Identity and authority

Include the passport biographical page used for travel, visa or entry documents applicable to the traveller, emergency contacts and any name-change evidence. A companion needs their own travel documents. If another person may obtain records, speak for the patient, handle payment or receive results, prepare separate authorisations for each role.

China's hospital record rules link a patient's medical-record identifier to identity information and require identity and authorisation documents when an agent requests copies [1]. Use the passport spelling consistently, but do not alter old source records. Instead, place former names, transliterations or number changes in an identity crosswalk.

Visa requirements vary by nationality, residence and application centre. The official Chinese visa service lists a valid passport, application material and, in some cases, prior-passport or name-change evidence among the basic documents [2]. Check the responsible embassy, consulate or visa centre for the current case rather than treating a hospital invitation as a visa guarantee.

#### 2. One-page clinical brief

The brief is a map, not a substitute for records. Include:

- confirmed and suspected diagnoses, with diagnosis dates;
- present symptoms, functional limits and recent change;
- major conditions, operations and complications;
- current treatment and the last treatment date;
- current medicines and allergies;
- the requested decision and no more than five priority questions;
- the author, source language and version date.

Use neutral wording. If the original says “cannot exclude,” the brief must not say “confirmed.” China's medical-record rules define records broadly to include text, charts, images and pathology and recognise both paper and electronic forms [1]. A patient-written summary is navigation; it is not the authoritative hospital record.

#### 3. Source evidence

Make a chronological evidence table with date, institution, document type, body site or specimen, and file name. Include the reports that can change the decision—not every routine page by default.

For imaging, provide the radiology report **and the complete DICOM study**. JPEG screenshots do not preserve the series, acquisition data or full image set needed for independent review. DICOM is the international standard for medical images and related information and defines exchangeable image formats with clinical data [3]. Test that each study opens, contains the expected series and belongs to the correct patient.

For pathology, distinguish the report, representative slide images, glass slides, paraffin blocks and unstained sections. Ask the reviewing laboratory what it actually accepts and how tissue should be shipped. Never relabel material yourself.

For laboratory data, include values, units, reference ranges, collection dates and trends. A translated value without its unit or range can be actively misleading.

#### 4. Treatment exposure

List operations, radiotherapy, systemic medicines, implanted devices, transfusions and rehabilitation as exposures, not as vague history. Depending on the case, record operation notes, final pathology, radiation plan and dose summary, drug generic names and cycles, dose changes, reactions, implant model/UDI, or functional outcome measures.

This section prevents a common failure: the current recommendation appears reasonable only because the reviewer cannot see what was already tried, stopped or complicated.

#### 5. Medicines, allergies and travel supply

Create one table with generic name, brand, strength, dosage form, route, dose, timing, indication, prescriber, start/stop plan and storage. Photographing loose tablets is not enough. Describe each allergy or adverse reaction: substance, reaction, severity, date and whether rechallenge occurred.

WHO identifies medication discrepancies as a recurring risk at transfers of care and calls for structured processes and better information [4]. CDC travel guidance likewise recommends a medical summary containing diagnoses, medicines by generic name and allergies [5].

Carry medicine in original labelled packaging when possible, with a clinician letter and prescription details. Restrictions differ by drug and jurisdiction; CDC specifically advises checking destination rules and documenting generic names, dose and indication [6]. Ask Chinese customs or the relevant diplomatic mission about controlled drugs, injectables, biological material or unusual quantities before travel. Do not assume that a prescription automatically permits import.

#### 6. Administrative and safety documents

Keep the hospital contact, department, campus, appointment or admission confirmation, itemised estimate, deposit receipt, insurer authorisation, policy/claim details, accommodation, emergency plan and return-travel flexibility in a non-clinical folder. An estimate is not clinical acceptance; an insurer guarantee is not a promise that every item will be covered.

CDC's medical-tourism guidance recommends pre-travel discussion, adequate regular medicines, planned home follow-up and complete overseas records [7]. Add a brief home-handoff note naming the clinician who will receive records after treatment; this makes the outbound packet part of a round trip rather than a one-way upload.

### Translate by decision value

Translate the one-page brief, current diagnostic conclusion, latest plan, medication/allergy table and the key reports required for the decision first. Keep a bilingual glossary for names, anatomy, diagnoses, drugs and procedures. A clinical reviewer should check numbers, laterality, negation, units and uncertainty.

Do not overwrite the original file with a translation. Pair them through an index such as `P03_pathology_2026-05-14_source.pdf` and `P03_pathology_2026-05-14_EN.pdf`. State who translated it and whether it is an informal aid, certified translation or hospital-issued bilingual document.

### Use a manifest and version control

The manifest is the packet's control panel. For each item, record:

| Field | Example purpose |
|---|---|
| packet version and date | prevents review of an obsolete upload |
| patient identity | detects mixed or mismatched records |
| file name and document date | makes chronology searchable |
| source institution and language | preserves provenance |
| original / translation / summary | prevents substitution |
| complete / pending / unavailable | exposes gaps honestly |
| checksum or file size | helps verify transfer integrity |

China's 2025 electronic-record notice requires controlled creation, storage, transmission and role-based access, and prohibits unauthorised viewing, copying or alteration [8]. Use the hospital's approved secure route. Avoid open links, shared group chats or a companion's personal cloud account for unrestricted clinical archives.

### Run the receiving-team test

Before booking non-refundable travel, ask the named contact to confirm:

1. the packet version received;
2. passport identity matched to the hospital record;
3. key PDFs and DICOM studies opened;
4. pathology or physical material requirements understood;
5. missing and pending items listed;
6. the clinical reviewer, department and next decision date identified.

If the answer is only “received, thanks,” the technical handoff may be complete but the clinical screen is not.

### Keep three copies, but one truth

Maintain an encrypted master, a travel-access copy and a minimal emergency set. The emergency set should contain the one-page summary, medicine/allergy table, important device information, emergency contacts and insurance details. Keep access instructions available to the patient and authorised companion without publishing the entire archive.

**Medical disclaimer:** This guide explains document organisation and transfer. It does not establish a diagnosis, guarantee hospital acceptance, satisfy every immigration or customs rule, or replace patient-specific advice from clinicians and authorities.

## FAQ

### Do I need to translate every medical record before travelling?

Usually not. Ask the reviewing department which records change the requested decision. Translate the current brief and key evidence first, while preserving all source documents for reference.

### Are radiology reports enough if I cannot obtain the images?

Not for an independent imaging review. Send the report and request the complete DICOM study. If it is unavailable, tell the hospital explicitly rather than substituting a few screenshots.

### Can my companion request records or sign forms for me?

Only within the authority recognised for that task. Record access, medical consent, communication and payment may require different documents; confirm hospital and legal requirements in advance.

### Should I send my whole passport and insurance policy by ordinary email?

Use the minimum necessary pages and the hospital's approved secure channel. Confirm the recipient, access period and deletion or retention process before sharing sensitive identity or health information.

### What is the final readiness check?

The receiving team can match identity, open the source evidence, understand the timeline, see medicine and allergy risks, identify missing items and state the next clinical decision. A neat folder alone is not readiness.
