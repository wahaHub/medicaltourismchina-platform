## Key Takeaways

- No single “China medical visa checklist” works for everyone. The embassy, consulate or visa centre handling your case sets the category and the document list.
- Ask for a truthful hospital letter with a narrow scope: patient identity, department, purpose, anticipated dates and contact. It supports the application; it cannot guarantee a visa or the final treatment.
- The patient and every companion each apply in their own name. Some posts ask for kinship, finance or hospital companion letters; others don't.
- Treat four fields as separate questions: application timing, visa validity for entry, number of entries and permitted duration of each stay.
- If care might run past the authorised stay, talk to the local exit-entry administration early. Extensions are fresh decisions by that office; hospitals don't grant them automatically.

## Content

A hospital can agree to review a case and the visa can still be refused. A visa can be issued and the hospital can still change the clinical plan after examining the patient. Neither decision binds the other: **two approvals, different authorities, different evidence**.

So a good visa plan doesn't start by promising a particular category. It builds a clean evidence chain — declared purpose of travel, hospital documents, finances, dates, companion arrangements — and it leaves the consular authority enough time to decide.

### Identify the responsible post first

Start with four facts: nationality, the country where you legally live now, passport type, and where the application has to be lodged. Apply outside your country of nationality and you may be asked for proof of lawful residence there. The Chinese Visa Application Service Center's basic list covers a passport, the application form/photo, local legal-stay evidence where applicable, and prior-passport or name-change material in relevant cases [1].

Work only from the current page of the embassy, consulate or authorised visa centre that will handle your case. Requirements published for another country make useful examples; they aren't transferable rules. Appointment method, biometrics, fees, processing options and supplementary evidence can all differ.

### Do not shop for a category by nickname

“Medical visa” is shorthand people use in conversation; it won't fill in an application form for you. Current official guidance from some Chinese missions routes short-term medical treatment, or accompanying a patient, through S2/private-affairs arrangements, but the supporting documents are post-specific [2]. The national application form itself lists medical treatment among “other personal matters” [3].

None of this lets a website, hospital or facilitator assign the final category. Describe the real activity, the expected length of stay, whether you are the patient or a companion, and the inviting institution. Then ask the responsible post which category and option to select.

Don't stretch visa-free entry or transit rules to cover a planned treatment stay. Eligibility, permitted area, onward-ticket conditions and allowed activities are decided separately from the hospital plan. Confirm the exact policy for your passport and itinerary before you rely on it.

### Make the hospital letter verifiable

Any required invitation, pre-admission or hospitalisation document should come from the hospital's authorised office, not an unrelated broker. Ideally it contains:

- the patient's name exactly as in the passport, date of birth, nationality and passport number;
- the hospital's legal name, campus, department and official contact;
- purpose and current status: consultation, evaluation, planned admission or ongoing treatment;
- anticipated arrival and clinical window, clearly marked as an estimate;
- companion name and role, included only if the hospital can truthfully confirm them;
- issue date, signer or office, and a way to verify the document.

Unless the hospital is authorised and able to substantiate it, the letter must not claim that treatment is guaranteed, medically successful, fully prepaid or finished by a fixed date. Keep the clinical detail to what the application requires — a general visa inbox doesn't need a full pathology archive.

### Keep the five evidence sets consistent

| Evidence set | What must agree |
|---|---|
| identity | passport name/number, prior names and application |
| purpose | application answers, hospital letter and itinerary |
| dates | intended entry, appointment window, accommodation and return plan |
| funding | bank/insurance/sponsor evidence and estimated costs |
| companion | separate passport/application, relationship and hospital role |

Some missions publish specific financial thresholds or companion limits for medical applications; others ask for different evidence. Presenting those local rules as a worldwide China requirement would be wrong. Current Pakistan mission guidance and other mission pages, for example, list medical-treatment requirements under S2, but applicants anywhere else must follow their own post's instructions [2].

Don't edit a hospital letter, don't recycle another patient's letter, don't invent a relationship. If dates change after the letter is issued, ask whether the hospital should reissue it and whether the post needs the update.

### Understand the four dates printed around a visa

#### 1. Application date

This is the day the post accepts your file. It says nothing about a decision arriving before your flight. Allow for appointments, supplementary documents, passport submission and public holidays.

#### 2. Validity for entry

This is the window during which you may enter. How long you may stay after arrival is a separate field.

#### 3. Number of entries

A single-entry visa can be used up by the first entry. A side trip outside mainland China mid-treatment can then break the whole schedule. Hong Kong and Macao run their own entry systems; a mainland visa doesn't necessarily cover them or preserve re-entry.

#### 4. Duration of each stay

Chinese regulations define duration of stay as the authorised time following each entry, counted from the day after entry [4]. Work out your departure deadline from the actual entry date and the wording on the visa or entry permission — the hospital's estimated discharge date doesn't set it.

Build a one-page date sheet: visa validity, entries, stay duration, planned arrival, accommodation, appointment, likely procedure, local recovery, the last safe date to apply for any extension, and the booked departure. The patient should read it too, not only the coordinator.

### Apply for companions as separate travellers

A caregiver's application doesn't ride along with the patient's. Check whether the post wants proof of relationship, a hospital-issued companion statement, shared funding or separate accommodation. State the companion's actual role — “medical escort,” relative, interpreter and paid carer mean different things.

Also plan for the case where the patient is approved and the companion isn't, or where their permitted stays differ. Hospital consent forms and record-access authorisations sit outside immigration documents entirely.

### Do not lock the trip before the passport returns

Until the visa is issued and the passport is physically back in hand, keep flights and accommodation changeable. A receipt, a preliminary online approval or an application appointment won't get anyone through the border. The hospital, for its part, should avoid fixing a procedure whose safe timing depends on examination, tests or pathology review.

Create a contingency table covering a delayed decision, a supplementary document request, a changed appointment, a passport held for processing, and outright refusal. For each row, name who contacts the post, the hospital, the airline, the insurer and the accommodation provider. An immigration delay is not a problem to hand to a clinician.

### On arrival, check the stay—not only the stamp

Confirm the entry date and the permitted stay straight away. Hotels normally take care of accommodation registration. A foreigner staying anywhere other than a hotel must register with the local authorities within 24 hours, personally or through the host, under National Immigration Administration guidance [5]. If the local process gives you a registration record, keep it.

Carry the passport or lawful travel/stay documentation as required, and keep secure copies. Being admitted to hospital suspends none of the immigration deadlines.

### If treatment runs longer, act before the deadline

Under National Immigration Administration guidance, an ordinary-visa holder who needs an extension applies to the competent local exit-entry administration, generally seven days before the authorised stay expires, with passport, application/photo and evidence supporting the reason [6]. Follow the local office's current instructions and appointment channel [7].

An extension can be approved, shortened or refused. The revised national regulation says an extension applies only to the current entry; entry count and entry validity stay as issued, and cumulative extensions are capped by the original authorised duration [4]. A new operation, a complication or delayed pathology does not automatically produce extra time, so don't promise it.

Ask what happens to the passport while the application is processed, and what the receipt can be used for locally. It may evidence lawful stay while the passport is held, but don't assume it works for air travel, for hotel check-in everywhere, or for cross-border movement.

### Close the immigration file at discharge

Before leaving China, reconcile the passport, visa/stay document, final departure deadline, tickets and any extension receipt. If the patient's condition affects flying, that is a medical and airline question to solve separately — permission to stay says nothing about fitness to fly.

Keep the hospital's final records apart from the visa application. The consular file proved the purpose at entry; the clinical file needs to show accurately what actually happened.

**Legal and medical disclaimer:** Visa and entry rules change, and they differ by nationality, residence, post and individual facts. This article is planning information only — not legal advice, and not a guarantee of visa issuance, entry, hospital acceptance or treatment duration. Confirm your own case with the responsible Chinese authority and the hospital.

## FAQ

### Which Chinese visa should a patient apply for?

Only the Chinese embassy, consulate or authorised visa centre responsible for your case can confirm the category. Some current mission guidance routes medical treatment through S2/private-affairs pathways, but documents and options vary by post.

### Is a hospital invitation letter enough to obtain a visa?

No. The letter supports your stated purpose; it doesn't replace the application, passport, identity, finance, residence, companion or any other evidence the deciding authority asks for.

### Can a hospital extend the patient's visa after surgery?

The hospital can provide medical evidence; the decision sits with the competent exit-entry administration. Apply before the stay expires, and plan for a refusal or a shorter period than you requested.

### Does visa validity show how long I may remain in China?

No. Entry validity, number of entries and duration of each stay are three separate fields. Work out the lawful departure date from your actual entry and the authorised stay.
