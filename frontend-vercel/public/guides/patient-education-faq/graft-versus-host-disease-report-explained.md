## Content

One patient's records may contain a low histologic grade from a bowel biopsy, a higher overall acute GVHD grade, a lung score of one, and almost complete donor chimerism. These results can appear contradictory when they are actually answering different questions. Before interpreting a number, identify whether it describes tissue injury, an organ, overall clinical severity, or the origin of blood-forming cells.

Patients need to understand what a report changes about care, rather than calculate every score themselves. For a consultation in China, preserve the report name, date, system used, and complete wording. Different assessment methods or time points should be reconciled before deciding that two reports show an actual change in disease.

## Place the report on the treatment timeline

Check the date of sampling or examination rather than only the date the report was printed. Identify whether it was obtained before treatment, after treatment began, during a taper, or around an infection. Without that context, results from separate stages can be combined into an inaccurate picture of the present condition.

A simple comparison can contain the date, original finding, and symptoms and medicines at the time. Identify the assessment that captured the worst episode and the one describing current status. Maximum acute GVHD grade and grade on a particular day can both be useful, but a historical peak is not automatically the patient's severity today.[1]

Reprinted records especially need their original examination dates preserved. A family may add an explanatory index while keeping the report unchanged. This makes a second opinion easier to conduct without asking the patient to reconstruct every event from memory during the appointment.

## Separate acute organ stage from overall clinical grade

Acute GVHD assessment records skin, liver, and upper and lower gastrointestinal involvement, then determines an overall clinical grade. It is not a simple sum of all the numbers and is not determined only by the most visible rash. MAGIC standardized the underlying observations, and the 2026 ASTCT guidance supports its grading framework.[1,2]

A patient with limited skin findings may still have clinically important gastrointestinal involvement. Ask which organ currently drives the overall assessment. Improvement of a rash alone is not enough for the patient to decide that immunosuppression can be substantially reduced.

The words grade and stage may also be translated inconsistently between documents. Preserve the original term and scoring system when possible. Comparing Roman numerals without knowing what they measure can create an apparent disagreement that disappears once the reports are read in full.

## Preserve the degree of certainty in pathology

Pathology may describe findings as not supporting GVHD, possible GVHD, or likely GVHD. Those qualifications communicate the strength of the tissue evidence. Symptoms, infections, medicines, and the sampling circumstances remain relevant to the clinical conclusion. A report cannot always be reduced to an unconditional yes or no.[3]

The degree of injury seen in sampled tissue is also different from overall clinical severity. A specimen represents one location at one time, influenced by sampling and existing treatment. A relatively low histologic grade alongside a higher clinical grade therefore does not necessarily mean that either document is mistaken.[1,3]

If a negative result does not explain significant continuing symptoms, ask the team what follows from the discrepancy. Do not independently stop treatment. Equally, a biopsy supporting GVHD does not remove the possibility of a simultaneous infection. Further investigation should be discussed in terms of the uncertainty it could resolve and its effect on management.

## Identify the organ behind a chronic GVHD score

The NIH framework scores several organs or sites and uses the number involved and severity to classify overall chronic GVHD. Lung involvement has a special role: a lung score of one can produce moderate overall disease, while a score of two or three can produce severe overall disease. One affected organ is therefore not always equivalent to mild overall GVHD.[4]

The classification is used after the chronic GVHD diagnosis is established. An abnormality entirely and unequivocally explained by another cause must be handled according to attribution rules; abnormalities with several contributors raise different considerations. Patients should not add every abnormality on a medical examination into a self-calculated GVHD score.[4]

Ask which organ most influences the treatment recommendation and how its score relates to function. The number provides a consistent record but cannot describe every difficulty with eating, reading, work, or movement. Those practical effects belong in the consultation even when they are not fully apparent in the summary grade.

## Do not equate a severity category with response

A change from severe to moderate deserves explanation, but that label alone does not calculate treatment success. NIH response criteria include organ and overall response definitions, and their role in research is not identical to individual clinical decision-making.[4,5]

A partial response generally requires a defined improvement without qualifying progression elsewhere. Improvement in one organ and worsening in another cannot be summarized by selecting only the favorable change. Reports or trials may use a mixed-response category, and the definition should be checked before interpreting the result.[5]

Conversely, a patient may function better before an objective score changes. Stability after a period of rapid deterioration can also matter to that person. The clinician considers trajectory, symptoms, and treatment burden alongside the formal criteria. Pain or functional difficulty should not be overlooked merely because a score appears unchanged.

## Read lung percentages as measurements, not remaining lung

FEV1, FVC, and percentages of predicted values describe particular lung-function measurements. They are not the percentage of lung tissue remaining or an individual survival probability. Interpretation requires the parameter, test quality, baseline, and assessment of infection or another pulmonary explanation.[6]

For example, a predicted-value percentage changing from 60% to 70% rises by ten percentage points. It should not be described loosely as the lung recovering by ten percent. This is an illustration of numerical language, not a declaration that a particular treatment-response threshold has been reached. The measure and conditions of the assessment still matter.

Bring complete lung-function reports for review in China, rather than a screenshot of the final comment. If testing methods or quality differ, the team should decide whether comparison is reliable. A recent reassuring result also does not remove the need to assess newly reduced exercise tolerance or breathlessness.

## Consider treatment effects when reading routine laboratory results

Low blood counts, liver or kidney changes, and electrolyte abnormalities deserve attention, but they should not all be translated as worsening GVHD. Recovery after transplantation, infection, medication, and other factors may contribute. Some repeated blood tests primarily monitor treatment safety rather than measure GVHD activity directly.[7]

Ruxolitinib labeling, for example, addresses blood-cell reductions and infection risk. A falling platelet count needs a clinical explanation and management decision; it is not an instruction for the family to continue unchanged, stop the medicine, or substitute another drug independently. Original units, reference ranges, and serial values support that discussion.[7]

Keep immune-suppressing medicines, anti-infective treatments, and other prescriptions on one list. This helps a clinician or pharmacist consider interactions and adverse effects. Brand names and formulations may differ between China and the home country, so packaging appearance or a similar-sounding name is not sufficient identification.

## Chimerism measures cell origin, not the absence of GVHD

Chimerism testing examines donor and recipient contributions to blood formation after transplantation. Results may differ between cell populations and change over time. This is a different question from whether tissue injury in the skin, bowel, or lungs is caused by GVHD.[8]

A high donor percentage cannot by itself exclude GVHD or establish that the original disease can never recur. Conversely, mixed chimerism or a changing proportion is not enough on its own to diagnose transplant failure. The underlying disease, tested cell population, method, and trend need interpretation by the transplant team.

When organizing records, identify blood versus marrow sampling and any selected cell population. Keep the complete report. Chimerism, testing for residual original disease, and GVHD organ scores should not be merged into a single supposed transplant recovery percentage, because they measure different processes.

## Keep biomarkers and patient questionnaires in their proper roles

Some blood algorithms provide additional risk information in acute GVHD. ASTCT's 2026 guidance distinguishes validated risk stratification from validated selection of first-line treatment for all patients.[2] A higher-risk result should lead to an explanation and monitoring discussion, not a self-directed increase in medication.

Patient questionnaires capture another dimension: dryness, pain, function, and the effect on daily life. A difference between the patient's rating and the clinician's score does not automatically mean exaggeration or an error; the assessments may address different aspects. NIH response work includes both clinician-assessed and patient-reported information.[5]

Complete questionnaires according to actual experience and describe recent change. Deliberately increasing symptoms to secure a treatment, or minimizing them to avoid more tests, reduces the usefulness of future comparisons. Consistent, accurate reporting is more helpful than trying to achieve a preferred score.

## Turn the interpretation into a next step

Bring a short list of unclear terms and ask the clinician to state the current diagnosis, important organs, unexplained abnormalities, and timing of reassessment. If hospitals disagree, compare dates, criteria, and available evidence before identifying the true clinical difference. International terminology guidance aims to reduce precisely this kind of confusion.[9]

After a Chinese review, retain the full written opinion and add new investigations and instructions to the existing timeline. A translation should keep phrases expressing uncertainty. A possible diagnosis must not become confirmed, and improvement in one organ must not become a claim of complete recovery throughout the body.

The practical value of a report lies in whether it supports observation, further testing, adjusted care, or reconsideration of treatment. Ask about that consequence. Any unresolved point should remain visible, with a named team responsible for obtaining the evidence or reassessing the problem.
