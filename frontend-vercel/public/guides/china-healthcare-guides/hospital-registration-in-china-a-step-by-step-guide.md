## Key Takeaways

- Booking reserves a visit; registration creates or activates the hospital encounter. Some hospitals combine them, while others require a separate on-site check-in.
- Register the patient under the exact passport identity—not a companion, translator or informal English nickname.
- Keep the hospital record number and use the same identity at every campus visit to avoid fragmented records.
- Confirm the department, clinic type, doctor, campus and date before paying. A successful payment to the wrong clinic does not solve a clinical mismatch.
- Do not share QR codes, full passport images or account passwords more widely than necessary.

## Content

In many Chinese hospitals, “registration” can mean three different actions: creating a patient record, buying a clinic registration, and reporting arrival so the patient enters the live queue. International patients often complete the first step at a service desk and the other two through a cashier, kiosk or phone.

National policy asks secondary and tertiary hospitals to provide appointment systems, several valid identity-document options and smoother check-in and payment routes.[1][2] The exact interface is local, so the safest approach is to understand what each confirmation proves.

## Step 1: Go to the Correct Hospital and Campus

Compare the appointment message with the hospital’s official name and address. Large hospital groups may have a main campus, an international building, a specialist branch and newer suburban sites.

At the entrance, show the Chinese department name and appointment code. If the patient has no appointment, ask for the **registration or patient service desk**, not simply the nearest clinical department.

## Step 2: Create or Retrieve the Patient Record

Present the passport or other document the hospital has agreed to accept. Staff may also request a telephone number, date of birth, sex, address or emergency contact.

Check the screen or printed record before leaving the desk:

- Passport name is complete and in the expected order
- Passport number is correct
- Date of birth is correct
- The record belongs to the patient, not the companion
- Previous hospital record number is linked if one exists

Hospitals are required to maintain numbered outpatient records and associate tests and imaging with the record.[3] A duplicate identity can separate today’s scan from last month’s consultation. If staff find two records, ask for the medical-record or information office to advise on merging; do not simply choose whichever number is easier.

## Step 3: Select the Department and Visit Type

Chinese registration menus may distinguish ordinary clinic, expert clinic, special-service clinic, international clinic, multidisciplinary clinic and return visit. Fees and access differ.

Use the referral question to choose. “Knee pain” may belong to sports medicine, joint surgery, rheumatology or rehabilitation depending on the history. If uncertain, ask an official triage desk. Do not pay extra for a senior title until the specialty is correct.

Confirm the doctor’s name, department, campus and date. Similar Chinese names can be easy to confuse in translation.

## Step 4: Pay the Registration Fee

Payment routes may include cashier, self-service machine, hospital app, mobile payment or an international department. Ask for a receipt showing the hospital and registered service.

An intermediary should never ask the patient to transfer a registration fee to a personal account without a hospital receipt. If insurance is expected to cover the visit, confirm whether registration is direct-billed or paid first and claimed later.

## Step 5: Report Arrival

Some systems place the patient in the queue after payment. Others require scanning a code, tapping “check in,” using a kiosk or reporting at the nurses’ desk. The appointment confirmation alone may not complete this step.

Look for a queue number and waiting area. Ask whether the number is displayed publicly, sent to the phone or called aloud. If the patient’s Roman-letter name is hard to hear, watch the screen and keep the number visible.

## Step 6: Fix Errors Before the Consultation

If the department, doctor or patient identity is wrong, go back to the service desk before entering the clinic. Ask whether the registration must be cancelled and repurchased or can be transferred. Keep cancellation and refund records.

Do not ask the doctor to use a record registered under someone else. That can place prescriptions and results into the wrong chart and create safety and privacy problems.

## Step 7: Protect the Patient Account

A hospital app or mini-program may contain appointments, invoices, diagnoses, prescriptions and results. Protect it as a medical record.

Share only what a coordinator needs. Crop booking screenshots when possible, mask unrelated passport information and never give a third party permanent access to the patient’s messaging or payment account. If an agent created the hospital account, obtain the username, recovery method and complete records before the relationship ends.

China’s health authorities require electronic medical-record access to be controlled by role and need and prohibit unauthorised viewing, copying, dissemination or alteration.[4] Patients should apply the same principle to their own devices.

## Step 8: Keep Proof of the Visit

After the consultation, save the registration receipt, outpatient note, orders, invoices and results under the same patient name and record number. These documents help with follow-up, reimbursement and correction of future identity errors.

If the hospital retains the electronic chart, the patient or authorised applicant can request copies of specified outpatient records and test materials under medical-record management rules.[3]

## A Five-Line Registration Note for the Phone

Keep this offline:

1. Patient passport name and number
2. Hospital and campus in Chinese
3. Department and doctor in Chinese
4. Appointment code and hospital record number
5. International desk or coordinator contact

This is usually enough to solve a check-in problem without exposing the rest of the medical file.

**Medical disclaimer:** Registration is an administrative step and does not determine whether a symptom is urgent. Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms require emergency assessment rather than waiting for ordinary registration.

## FAQ

### Is registration the same as making an appointment?

Not always. An appointment reserves a slot; registration may create the patient chart, purchase the clinic visit or confirm arrival. Some hospitals combine these steps.

### Can I register using my companion’s Chinese phone number?

Sometimes a local number can receive messages, but the patient identity must still be the patient’s. Make sure the patient can retrieve confirmations and results later.

### What if the system cannot read my passport name?

Use the hospital’s international or manual registration desk. Do not shorten the name or invent an identity without staff recording how it maps to the passport.

### Why do I have two hospital record numbers?

Different spelling, documents or campuses may have created duplicates. Ask the hospital’s record or information office whether the charts can be linked or merged.

### Can an agent keep control of my hospital account?

The patient should retain access. Obtain the login or recovery method and copies of all confirmations, records and invoices, then remove unnecessary third-party access.
