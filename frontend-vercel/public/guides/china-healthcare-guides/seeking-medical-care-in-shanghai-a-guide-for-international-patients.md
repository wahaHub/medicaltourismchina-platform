## Key Takeaways

- Shanghai offers ordinary public outpatient care, international or VIP services within public hospitals, and private international hospitals or clinics. The right route depends on clinical fit, language, price, insurance and after-hours support—not nationality alone.[1][3]
- Use the same passport spelling and identity number every time. A second hospital card or digital profile can split the record of one episode of care.
- The municipal “Suishenban” app supports foreign-passport registration for specific services, and Shanghai’s official hospital guide describes appointment booking through its healthcare section.[2][4]
- A direct-billing logo is not an approval. Confirm the facility, department, date of service, deductible and pre-authorisation with both the provider and insurer.
- For a medical emergency in Shanghai, call 120. The city’s official guide says the 120 service is available in English and asks callers to give a precise address and symptoms.[5]

## Content

Shanghai can feel easy to navigate because international clinics, large teaching hospitals and digital services are all visible online. The difficulty lies in choosing the correct layer of that system.

A traveller with an uncomplicated infection, a resident needing ongoing primary care and a patient flying in for complex surgery do not need the same entrance. Before comparing hospital names, decide which care pathway fits the present problem.

## Choose One of Three Care Pathways

### Ordinary public outpatient service

This can be appropriate when the relevant department is clear and the patient can manage a largely Chinese workflow or has reliable interpretation. It may offer direct access to a broad specialist network, but registration, payment, testing and pharmacy steps can occur at separate counters or in separate apps.

### International or VIP service in a public hospital

This route may add bilingual coordination, a different booking channel, longer consultation time, private rooms or commercial-insurance support. It does not necessarily cover every department, doctor, test or emergency service available elsewhere in the hospital. Ask whether care stays within the international service or moves into the standard system for imaging, pathology, surgery or admission.

### Private international hospital or clinic

This may suit primary care, paediatrics, routine specialist care or patients whose insurer has an established network. Before using it for a complex episode, confirm inpatient capability, intensive-care backup, pathology and imaging access, blood-bank arrangements and transfer protocol.

Shanghai’s municipal portal publishes an international medical-services directory with addresses, opening hours and contact details.[1] Treat it as a navigation aid, not a quality ranking or guarantee that a listed provider manages the patient’s condition.

## Test the Match Before Paying for Travel

Send a compact case brief to the actual receiving service. It should state the working diagnosis, the decision sought, current symptoms, major prior treatment, medication and allergy risks, and what source materials are available.

Then ask for a written reply to four questions:

1. Which department and service pathway should receive the patient?
2. Has a clinician reviewed the records, or has only an administrator checked them?
3. What must be repeated after arrival, and why?
4. What would make the visit inappropriate, urgent or impossible to complete on the planned dates?

An invitation letter, quotation or appointment does not establish that a procedure is medically indicated. A preliminary opinion may change after examination, local imaging, pathology review or new laboratory results.

## Make the Digital Identity Match the Passport

Shanghai’s official guide to local hospitals describes registration with a passport, creation of a hospital card for a first visit and online appointment booking through the healthcare section of “Suishenban.”[2] The city’s guide to the Shanghai QR Code states that foreign-passport users can register, with manual identity review for some functions.[4]

Before relying on the app, test it while there is time to recover from a failed verification. Keep these fields consistent:

- surname and given names in passport order;
- passport number, including letters and leading zeros;
- nationality and date of birth;
- mobile number that can receive verification codes;
- hospital patient number once issued.

Do not create a new patient casually because an old profile will not open. Ask the hospital whether it can locate or merge the existing record. A broken identity chain can leave the consultation in one profile and the imaging or laboratory result in another.

## Keep an Offline Appointment Card

For every visit, store a plain-text card containing:

| Field | Example of the needed detail |
|---|---|
| Hospital | Official Chinese name, not an informal translation |
| Site | District, campus and street address |
| Clinic | Department plus ordinary, VIP or international service |
| Visit | Date, time window and registration status |
| Patient | Passport name and hospital number |
| Contact | Hospital telephone and named coordinator, if any |
| Preparation | Fasting, medication or specimen instructions confirmed by the clinical team |

Shanghai hospitals can have sites on opposite sides of the city. Search the precise address, not only the institution name. Allow for security, registration and travel between buildings if tests are scheduled on the same day.

## Plan What Happens After the Doctor Says “Get a Test”

In a public-hospital visit, the doctor’s order is often the midpoint rather than the end. The patient may need to pay, find a testing area, book a slot, return for the result and then obtain a second clinical review. Shanghai’s municipal step-by-step guide describes registration, consultation, payment, tests and pharmacy as distinct stages.[2]

Before leaving the consultation room, ask:

- Is the test performed today or by appointment?
- Must payment be completed before the booking becomes valid?
- Where will the result appear, and under which patient number?
- Does the doctor need to review it in person?
- Who acts if the result is urgent?

An English-speaking doctor does not guarantee English instructions at every counter. Put the test name, preparation and location in Chinese on the patient’s phone or ask for a bilingual escort.

## Price the Pathway, Not Just the Consultation

The city’s comparison of public and private hospitals notes different service and cost patterns and advises advance appointments for scarce specialist slots.[3] The registration fee is only a small part of an international patient’s total exposure.

Request estimates for the exact pathway, including:

- ordinary, VIP or international consultation;
- repeat imaging, laboratory or pathology review;
- professional interpretation;
- hospital deposit and room type;
- physician, anaesthesia, device and medicine charges;
- companion accommodation and extended stay;
- translation and record export after discharge.

For insurance, ask the provider to submit the intended diagnosis, date, campus and service category. Ask the insurer whether direct billing covers only approved items and whether the patient must pay deposits or exclusions. Save both answers. “We work with your insurer” and “your claim is authorised” are different statements.

## Treat International Service Labels as a Starting Point

Shanghai introduced a local specification for international medical services in 2024, covering areas such as staffing, quality management and payment models.[7] That is useful context, but a label still does not tell a patient which clinician is available next month or whether a particular test is included.

Ask operational questions that can be checked:

- Who is clinically responsible for the case?
- Is the interpreter trained for consent and medication discussions?
- Which records will be provided in English?
- Is there a 24-hour clinical contact after a procedure?
- If the patient transfers, who sends the handover and images?

## Know What to Do in an Emergency

Dial 120 for a medical emergency in Shanghai. The city’s emergency guide says the 120 line is available in English and advises callers to give the exact location, the patient’s condition and a callback number, then send someone to guide the ambulance and clear access.[5]

The same guide notes that callers may be asked about the destination hospital. In a time-critical situation, follow dispatch guidance and prioritise an appropriate nearby emergency facility rather than insisting on a distant elective-care provider. Carry the passport, medication list, major allergy information and insurance details if they can be gathered without delaying departure.

Shanghai 12345 is a 24-hour government-service channel offered in Chinese and multiple foreign languages.[6] It can help with non-emergency city-service questions; it is not for medical triage and should not delay a 120 call.

## Build the Exit Packet Before Leaving Shanghai

Do not wait until the airport to discover that imaging is only visible inside an app. Ask for:

- the consultation and discharge summary;
- final medication list with start, stop and duration instructions;
- laboratory results and pathology report;
- DICOM imaging plus the radiology report;
- operative or procedure note when applicable;
- restrictions, warning signs and emergency instructions;
- follow-up interval and a contact who can receive the home clinician’s questions;
- itemised invoice and proof of payment.

Check that every page belongs to the patient and that dates, laterality and specimen identifiers are correct. Translate the final—not preliminary—record used for continuing care.

## A Two-Day Practical Plan

**The day before:** reopen the appointment, confirm the campus, check fasting instructions, test the payment method, charge the phone and send the interpreter the purpose of the visit.

**The day of care:** bring the passport and offline appointment card; note the patient number; photograph no clinical screen without permission; ask how results return to the doctor; collect receipts before changing buildings.

**After the visit:** compare the medicine list with what the patient was taking before, download results while access works, book any review, and send a structured update to the clinician at home.

**Medical disclaimer:** This guide explains common navigation and planning issues in Shanghai. It does not select a provider, establish a diagnosis or replace an individual medical assessment. Service availability and digital access can change. For a medical emergency in Shanghai, call 120.

## FAQ

### Should an international patient use a public or private hospital in Shanghai?

There is no automatic answer. Compare the needed specialty, language support, inpatient and emergency backup, timing, cost and insurer network. A public hospital’s international service can also be a distinct third pathway.[1][3]

### Can a foreign passport be used in Suishenban?

Shanghai’s current guidance says foreign-passport users can register for specific services, with manual identity review in some cases.[4] Test the account before the appointment and keep the hospital’s direct booking route as a fallback.

### Why does the hospital ask me to make a new medical card?

Hospitals commonly create their own patient record for first visits.[2] Ask whether an earlier number exists before accepting another one; duplicate profiles can split results and follow-up.

### Does direct billing mean I will pay nothing?

No. Direct billing can still leave a deductible, co-payment, deposit, excluded service or amount above the policy limit. The insurer must authorise the specific provider and episode.

### Is 12345 the number for an ambulance in Shanghai?

No. Call 120 for a medical emergency. Shanghai 12345 handles non-emergency government-service enquiries and is available in multiple languages.[5][6]
