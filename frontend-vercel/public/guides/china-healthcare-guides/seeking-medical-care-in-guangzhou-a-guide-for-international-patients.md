## Content

Guangzhou handles referrals from across the region and doubles as an everyday care base for people living, working or travelling in the Greater Bay Area. Its patient mix is wide, and its booking routes are easy to get wrong.

The city has national, provincial, university-affiliated, municipal and district institutions. Some sit inside Guangzhou's unified appointment service, some put more functions in their own WeChat account, and some run international services through a separate desk. What a patient needs is one coherent route through these layers — five half-finished registrations help nobody.

## Define the Episode of Care

Start by working out which kind of episode this is:

- a same-week outpatient problem;
- a specialist opinion based on existing records;
- planned treatment requiring pre-admission review;
- continuing care for a person who lives in Guangzhou;
- urgent deterioration that should bypass routine booking.

Write down what decision you need from this visit and how long it can safely wait. A stable second-opinion request can sit in the queue for record review. Chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, new weakness or reduced consciousness cannot — those belong in the emergency system today.

## Verify the Institution Before Comparing “International” Services

The Guangzhou Municipal Health Commission's service portal carries current hospital lists, medical-institution information and professional or licence searches.[2] Check there first, then move on to the institution's official site or verified account.

Note down these identifiers:

| Identifier | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Legal institution name | Separates the licensed hospital from an agency or similarly named clinic |
| Campus address | Prevents arrival at the wrong site |
| Department and subspecialty | Confirms clinical fit |
| Outpatient category | Ordinary, expert, premium or international services can have different rules |
| Registration identity | Passport, foreign permanent resident ID card or on-site hospital number |
| Result-review route | Determines whether a second appointment is needed |
| Emergency capability | Must be confirmed separately from routine clinic hours |

Guangzhou announced an international medical-tourism service centre working with 17 pilot hospitals in 2026.[7] Treat the “pilot” label as a sign that services are still being built out. It tells you nothing about whether a particular team, treatment, bed or insurer is available for your case.

## Use Guangzhou Health Link Without Losing the Patient Identity

According to the city health commission's Guangzhou Health Link FAQ, overseas users can register with a passport, name and mobile number. The FAQ lists the unified website, the official WeChat account, a mini program, the Suikangban (“Suihaoban”) app and the 12320 booking hotline as appointment channels.[3]

The municipal government has also announced passport-like online access for holders of the Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card, initially across 17 institutions in 2024.[1] The two identity routes are related but not identical. Use whichever document the hospital confirms it can accept, then stick with that same document for the whole episode.

A few habits that prevent problems:

- enter the passport name exactly as printed;
- keep the booking mobile active for verification messages;
- save the hospital patient number after the first visit;
- never bind one person under two spellings;
- ask before creating a second record when a system says “patient not found”;
- keep a plain-text copy of the appointment alongside the screenshot.

Even with the unified platform, you still have to read the hospital's own instructions. A booking can carry conditions of its own: payment, on-site identity verification, or arrival before a cut-off time.

## Treat Language as a Clinical-Safety Task

Over the course of one visit you may pass through Mandarin, Cantonese and English. A registration clerk who speaks some English may still struggle to interpret a diagnosis, a risk percentage or a medication change accurately.

The municipal 960169 platform interprets for public and government-service enquiries and can connect with 12320, 120 and other hotlines in a three-way call.[5] The published guide dates from 2022, so confirm current hours and language coverage before counting on it. For a planned consultation, bring in a medical interpreter who will:

- interpret everything in the first person;
- carry uncertainty across faithfully, without smoothing it over;
- read drug names, doses and allergies accurately;
- remain for consent, test instructions and discharge;
- disclose conflicts if employed by an agency selling the treatment.

Carry a small bilingual card listing the diagnosis, serious allergies, high-risk medicines, mobility needs and an emergency contact. The full record still travels with you; the card just gets the right facts across at triage.

## Organise the First Visit Around Results

Bring a one-page summary plus a dated index of the source record, and keep administrative files apart from clinical ones. For imaging, carry the DICOM data as well as the reports. If the problem is cancer or a rare disease, ask before travelling whether the receiving team wants pathology slides, blocks, or reports alone.

During the consultation, ask the doctor to mark which tests are:

- required before a decision;
- optional or conditional;
- repeats of existing tests and the reason for repeating them;
- likely to return the same day;
- likely to require a separate review.

Before you leave the department, pin down three things: where the result will appear, who books the review, and what happens if a critical value comes back. Patients whose Chinese phone number or app access may lapse after leaving the country need this most.

## Understand Guangzhou’s Three-Day Follow-up Rule

Guangzhou's 2026 official Q&A lays out the provincial “one registration valid for three days” measure. When pending test results stop an ordinary outpatient episode from being completed, the institution can arrange one no-fee follow-up—generally within three days—to interpret the results and decide the next clinical step.[4]

It works only within set boundaries, and the city spells them out:

- it applies to ordinary outpatient care at public medical institutions;
- the original episode must remain incomplete because results are pending;
- the institution arranges the follow-up according to result timing and clinician availability;
- emergency, fever, cross-department or cross-specialty visits are excluded;
- premium services and cases already completed at the first visit fall outside it;
- the same doctor may not be available, although the same team or department can continue care.

Ask at the triage desk before you leave: “Does this test qualify for the three-day follow-up, and where will the follow-up number appear?” Assume nothing — the system may not create it on its own.

## Plan Payments Across More Than One Counter

Confirm whether registration is paid online or on arrival, whether the international or premium service prices separately, and whether tests require prepayment. Carry a second payment method too — a foreign card or app can fail at a particular terminal.

For commercial insurance, obtain two written confirmations:

1. the hospital confirms whether it can bill the insurer for this campus and service;
2. the insurer confirms authorisation, deductible, exclusions, limit and deposit requirements for this episode.

A verbal estimate from a coordinator settles nothing. Ask for itemised charges, and keep electronic invoices, receipts, prescriptions and discharge documents under the same identity name as the policy.

## Know the Emergency Route

In Guangzhou, an ambulance means 120. The city's international safety page lists 120 for ambulance service and says emergency services can take calls in English.[6] Give the district, street, building, floor or gate, a callback number, the patient's age, the main symptom and the level of consciousness.

While the patient is unstable, skip the routine outpatient desk and any talk of negotiating a distant destination; the emergency system decides by time and capability. If language blocks the call and circumstances allow, Guangzhou's published 960169 service can set up three-way interpretation with 120. In a life-threatening emergency, though, the first action is still dialling 120.[5]

## Prepare the Handover Beyond Guangzhou

Before discharge or the trip home, line the new medicine list up against the old one and account for every change. Collect the final or working diagnosis, completed and pending results, procedure notes, warning signs, activity and travel restrictions, follow-up timing and a clinical contact.

Anyone continuing care elsewhere in the Greater Bay Area or overseas should ask whether image links require a Chinese mobile number and when they expire. Export the DICOM data and PDFs before leaving Guangzhou, then open them on a second device to make sure they work.

## FAQ

### Can an overseas visitor register on Guangzhou Health Link with a passport?

Yes, according to the city health commission's current FAQ: overseas users register with a passport, name and mobile number.[3] The hospital may still ask for on-site verification or issue its own patient number.

### Is Guangzhou Health Link the only booking channel?

No. The official FAQ lists the unified website, the WeChat account, a mini program, the Suihaoban app and 12320, and hospitals often run their own verified channels on top of those.[3]

### Does one registration really cover three days?

Only in defined circumstances. When pending test results stop an ordinary public outpatient visit from being completed, the registration can carry one no-fee follow-up. Emergency, fever, premium and cross-specialty care fall outside it.[4]

### Can I use 960169 as my medical interpreter?

It is a public-service interpretation platform that can support calls to certain hotlines.[5] Clinical visits, consent and discharge call for an interpreter with medical competence and a clear confidentiality role.

### Which number should I call for an ambulance in Guangzhou?

Call 120, give the exact location and describe the patient's condition. The city's international safety page says the emergency service can respond in English.[6]
