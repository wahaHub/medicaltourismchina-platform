## Content

Guangzhou is both a major referral city and a practical care base for people living, working or travelling across the Greater Bay Area. That makes its patient population diverse—and its booking routes easy to misunderstand.

The city has national, provincial, university-affiliated, municipal and district institutions. Some appear in Guangzhou’s unified appointment service; some place more functions in their own WeChat account; some international services use a separate desk. A patient needs one coherent route through these layers, not five partially completed registrations.

## Define the Episode of Care

Begin by deciding what kind of episode this is:

- a same-week outpatient problem;
- a specialist opinion based on existing records;
- planned treatment requiring pre-admission review;
- continuing care for a person who lives in Guangzhou;
- urgent deterioration that should bypass routine booking.

Write down the decision required from the visit and the safe time window. A stable second-opinion request can wait for record review; chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, new weakness or reduced consciousness should not be routed through a future appointment.

## Verify the Institution Before Comparing “International” Services

The Guangzhou Municipal Health Commission’s service portal provides current hospital lists, medical-institution information and professional or licence searches.[2] Start there, then open the institution’s official site or verified account.

Record these identifiers:

| Identifier | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Legal institution name | Separates the licensed hospital from an agency or similarly named clinic |
| Campus address | Prevents arrival at the wrong site |
| Department and subspecialty | Confirms clinical fit |
| Outpatient category | Ordinary, expert, premium or international services can have different rules |
| Registration identity | Passport, foreign permanent resident ID card or on-site hospital number |
| Result-review route | Determines whether a second appointment is needed |
| Emergency capability | Must be confirmed separately from routine clinic hours |

Guangzhou announced an international medical-tourism service centre working with 17 pilot hospitals in 2026.[7] A “pilot” label may indicate service development; it does not prove that a particular team, treatment, bed or insurer is available for an individual patient.

## Use Guangzhou Health Link Without Losing the Patient Identity

The city health commission’s Guangzhou Health Link FAQ says overseas users can register with a passport, name and mobile number. It lists the unified website, official WeChat account, mini program, Suikangban (“Suihaoban”) app and 12320 booking hotline as appointment channels.[3]

The municipal government also announced passport-like online access for holders of the Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card, initially across 17 institutions in 2024.[1] These are related but not identical identity routes. Choose the document the hospital confirms it can use and keep it unchanged throughout the episode.

Practical safeguards:

- enter the passport name exactly as printed;
- keep the booking mobile active for verification messages;
- save the hospital patient number after the first visit;
- do not bind one person under multiple spellings;
- ask before creating a second record when a system says “patient not found”;
- save the appointment as text as well as a screenshot.

The unified platform does not remove the need to check the hospital’s own instructions. A booking may still require payment, on-site identity verification or arrival before a cut-off time.

## Treat Language as a Clinical-Safety Task

Guangzhou patients may encounter Mandarin, Cantonese and English at different points. A bilingual registration clerk is not automatically qualified to interpret a diagnosis, risk percentage or medication change.

The municipal 960169 platform offers interpretation for public and government-service enquiries and can connect with 12320, 120 and other hotlines in a three-way call.[5] Because the published guide dates from 2022, verify current hours and language availability before relying on it. For a planned consultation, arrange a medical interpreter who can:

- interpret everything in the first person;
- preserve uncertainty rather than summarising it away;
- read drug names, doses and allergies accurately;
- remain for consent, test instructions and discharge;
- disclose conflicts if employed by an agency selling the treatment.

Prepare a small bilingual card with the diagnosis, serious allergies, high-risk medicines, mobility needs and emergency contact. It is not a substitute for the full record, but it helps at triage.

## Organise the First Visit Around Results

Bring a one-page summary and a dated index of the source record. Separate administrative files from clinical files. For imaging, carry DICOM data and reports; for cancer or rare disease, confirm whether the receiving team wants pathology slides, blocks or only reports before travelling.

During the consultation, ask the doctor to mark which tests are:

- required before a decision;
- optional or conditional;
- repeats of existing tests and the reason for repeating them;
- likely to return the same day;
- likely to require a separate review.

Before leaving the department, find out where the result will appear, who books the review, and what happens if a critical result is found. This is particularly important for patients whose phone number or app access may stop working after leaving China.

## Understand Guangzhou’s Three-Day Follow-up Rule

Guangzhou’s 2026 official Q&A explains the provincial “one registration valid for three days” measure. When an ordinary outpatient episode cannot be completed because test results are pending, the institution can arrange one no-fee follow-up—generally within three days—for result interpretation and the next clinical action.[4]

This is not a transferable three-day pass. The city lists important boundaries:

- it applies to ordinary outpatient care at public medical institutions;
- the original episode must remain incomplete because results are pending;
- the institution arranges the follow-up according to result timing and clinician availability;
- emergency, fever, cross-department or cross-specialty visits are excluded;
- premium services and cases completed at the first visit are not covered;
- the same doctor may not be available, although the same team or department can continue care.

Ask at the triage desk before leaving: “Does this test qualify for the three-day follow-up, and where will the follow-up number appear?” Do not assume the system will create it automatically.

## Plan Payments Across More Than One Counter

Confirm whether registration is paid online or on arrival, whether the international or premium service has separate pricing, and whether tests require prepayment. Carry a second payment method in case a foreign card or app is not accepted at a particular terminal.

For commercial insurance, obtain two written confirmations:

1. the hospital confirms whether it can bill the insurer for this campus and service;
2. the insurer confirms authorisation, deductible, exclusions, limit and deposit requirements for this episode.

A coordinator’s verbal estimate is not an invoice. Ask for itemised charges and keep electronic invoices, receipts, prescriptions and discharge documents in the same identity name used for the policy.

## Know the Emergency Route

Call 120 for an ambulance in Guangzhou. The city’s international safety page lists 120 for ambulance service and states that emergency services can respond to calls in English.[6] Give the district, street, building, floor or gate, a callback number, the patient’s age, main symptom and level of consciousness.

Do not call a routine outpatient desk to negotiate a distant destination while the patient is unstable. Let the emergency system prioritise time and capability. If language blocks the call and circumstances allow, Guangzhou’s published 960169 service describes three-way interpretation with 120—but the first action in a life-threatening emergency remains calling 120.[5]

## Prepare the Handover Beyond Guangzhou

Before discharge or return travel, reconcile the new medicine list against the old one. Obtain the final diagnosis or working diagnosis, completed and pending results, procedure notes, warning signs, activity and travel restrictions, follow-up timing and a clinical contact.

For patients continuing care elsewhere in the Greater Bay Area or overseas, ask whether image links require a Chinese mobile number and when they expire. Export DICOM data and PDFs while still in Guangzhou, then check the files on a second device.

## FAQ

### Can an overseas visitor register on Guangzhou Health Link with a passport?

The current city health-commission FAQ says overseas users can register using a passport, name and mobile number.[3] A hospital may still require on-site verification or its own patient number.

### Is Guangzhou Health Link the only booking channel?

No. The official FAQ lists the unified website, WeChat account, mini program, Suihaoban app and 12320, while hospitals may also maintain their own verified channels.[3]

### Does one registration really cover three days?

Only in defined circumstances. It can provide one no-fee follow-up when pending tests prevent an ordinary public outpatient visit from being completed. Emergency, fever, premium and cross-specialty care are excluded.[4]

### Can I use 960169 as my medical interpreter?

It is a public-service interpretation platform and can support calls to certain hotlines.[5] For a clinical visit, consent or discharge, arrange an interpreter with medical competence and a clear confidentiality role.

### Which number should I call for an ambulance in Guangzhou?

Call 120 and state the exact location and patient condition. Guangzhou’s international safety page says the emergency service can respond in English.[6]
