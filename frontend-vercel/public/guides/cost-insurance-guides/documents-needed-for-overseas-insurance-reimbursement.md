## Key Takeaways

- There is no universal claim list. Obtain the insurer’s current checklist, translation standard, submission deadline and original-document rule before treatment.
- A complete claim proves five separate facts: who was treated, why care was needed, what was delivered, what was charged and who actually paid.
- Keep the official medical fee receipt and detailed charge list together. One proves the formal charge/payment; the other explains the services, medicines and consumables.
- Preserve Chinese source documents and add translations as separate, labeled files. Never replace codes, amounts or issuing details with an English rewrite.
- Track every requested item as final, preliminary, amended, missing or not applicable, and keep insurer requests and submission receipts.

## Content

A folder containing many hospital PDFs can still fail as a claim. The insurer must be able to move from the insured person to the diagnosis, from the diagnosis to treatment, from treatment to each charge and from each charge to a verified payment.

### Start with the insurer’s evidence contract

Before admission, request a written checklist that states:

- claim form and signature rules;
- claimant and patient identity documents;
- pre-authorization or referral evidence;
- required clinical records;
- invoice, receipt and itemized-bill requirements;
- proof-of-payment standard;
- translation and certification rules;
- whether original paper documents must be mailed;
- accepted formats and file limits;
- claim currency and bank information;
- submission deadline and time zone;
- consent for the insurer to contact the hospital.

Save the checklist version and date. A call-centre agent’s informal list can become outdated; the policy and claims instructions control.

Insurance-conduct rules differ by jurisdiction, but the FCA’s current claims-handling rules provide a useful benchmark: insurers should handle claims promptly and fairly, give reasonable guidance and appropriate progress information, and settle promptly after terms are agreed [1]. Keep every request and response so delay can be traced to a missing item or decision owner.

### Build the claim in seven evidence layers

**1. Identity and eligibility**

- passport identity page;
- insurance card/certificate and policy number;
- relationship to policyholder, if different;
- hospital patient and encounter numbers;
- travel dates when relevant;
- claimant identity and authority.

Names must match or be explained. If the hospital system reverses name order or omits a middle name, obtain a hospital identity-link statement rather than editing documents.

**2. Authorization and referral**

- referral letter;
- insurer pre-authorization;
- guarantee-of-payment versions;
- second-opinion approval;
- approved diagnosis, service, dates, facility and limit;
- amendments after the clinical plan changed.

**3. Medical necessity**

- consultation/assessment note;
- confirmed or working diagnosis;
- pathology, imaging and relevant laboratory reports;
- reason for admission or procedure;
- prior conservative treatment if required by policy;
- clinician’s explanation of why the service was indicated.

**4. Care actually delivered**

- discharge summary;
- procedure and anesthesia record;
- treatment administration record or cycle summary;
- implant/device record;
- prescriptions and take-home medicine list;
- rehabilitation or follow-up attendance where claimed.

**5. Charges**

- detailed charge list with item names/codes, dates, units, quantities and amounts;
- separate professional, pharmacy, laboratory or device bills;
- final settlement statement;
- removed, reversed or refunded items.

**6. Payment**

- official medical fee receipt;
- deposit and top-up proofs;
- card/bank/mobile-payment record;
- refund or reversal evidence;
- final patient-paid amount;
- currency and transaction reference.

**7. Submission and communication**

- completed claim form;
- translations;
- document manifest;
- upload/mail receipt;
- insurer case number;
- requests for additional information;
- adjudication and appeal correspondence.

### Pair the receipt with the detail

China’s medical-fee receipt rules recognize the medical receipt as a valid reimbursement document [2]. The national electronic-receipt reform also introduced unified outpatient/inpatient receipt formats and a supporting electronic fee-detail format [3].

Use them together:

- **official medical fee receipt:** formal issuer, payer/patient information, total and payment categories;
- **detailed charge list:** clinical service, medicine and consumable lines behind the total;
- **payment proof:** evidence money left the payer and reached the hospital;
- **settlement/refund:** evidence the account ended at the claimed net amount.

If the patient paid several deposits, the bank total may exceed the final expense until a refund is processed. Claim the reconciled net patient cost and include the refund record.

### Request medical records on a production calendar

Do not wait until departure to ask for every record. Use three dates:

- available during care;
- available at discharge;
- completed later after final pathology, coding or record sign-off.

China’s medical-record rules allow patients or authorized applicants to request copies of specified materials including admission records, orders, laboratory and imaging reports, consent forms, surgery/anesthesia records, pathology, nursing and discharge records [4]. The current rules also allow completed portions to be copied before the full record is finished, with later-completed parts supplied afterward [5].

Ask which office provides certified copies, what identity/authorization is required, what fee applies and how records can be delivered abroad. Maintain a pending-item owner and expected date.

### Translate without destroying provenance

For each source file preserve:

- Chinese original;
- hospital English summary, if issued;
- third-party translation, if required;
- translator declaration/certification where requested;
- one-to-one filename mapping.

Use a bilingual glossary for recurring hospital terms, but do not silently convert a diagnosis code, drug strength, device model or amount. Mark handwritten or illegible content as such instead of guessing.

Ask the insurer whether it accepts hospital-produced English summaries, ordinary translations or only certified translations. Translate only the required scope after confirming; full-record translation can be expensive and unnecessary.

### Build a manifest with document states

| ID | Document | Issuer | Service date | Version | Language | Claim purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use statuses:

`requested → received preliminary → received final → translated → submitted → accepted / replacement requested`

Mark amended reports. Never delete the earlier version; link the amendment to the claim and explain which controls.

For each insurer request, record the exact missing field—not merely “medical records incomplete.” This allows the hospital to answer precisely.

### Reconcile the claim before submission

Run six checks:

1. **identity:** passport, policy and hospital records refer to the same person;
2. **timeline:** consultation, admission, service and discharge dates are plausible;
3. **clinical:** diagnosis and records support the claimed service;
4. **quantity:** procedure, doses, days and devices match the charge list;
5. **financial:** receipts, payment proofs, refunds and claimed total reconcile;
6. **authorization:** final care fits the approval or includes an amendment.

Create a one-page exception note for any mismatch. Do not alter source documents to make them agree.

### Submit a navigable package

Recommended order:

1. claim cover sheet and case number;
2. manifest;
3. claim form and authorization;
4. identity/eligibility;
5. clinical summary and diagnosis evidence;
6. treatment records;
7. itemized bill and receipt;
8. payment/refund proof;
9. translations paired with sources;
10. exception explanations.

Use searchable PDFs when available, page numbers and file sizes within portal limits. Protect the archive with encryption and send passwords through a separate channel if accepted. China’s 2025 electronic-medical-record guidance reiterates that patient record information must be protected and not disclosed through improper online channels [6].

### Respond to additional-information requests surgically

Ask the insurer to identify:

- claim line or benefit under review;
- document or field missing;
- reason it is material;
- accepted alternative evidence;
- response deadline;
- whether undisputed lines can be paid meanwhile.

Send a versioned supplement with a mini-manifest. Do not resend the entire unindexed folder each time.

When a claim is reduced or denied, request the line-level calculation, policy clause, exchange rate, deductible/coinsurance, excluded item, clinical rationale if applicable and appeal deadline. The appeal pack should answer that reason directly.

### Close the file after adjudication

Reconcile:

`eligible amount − deductible − copay/coinsurance − limits = insurer payment`

Then match bank receipt, hospital refunds and any direct-billing payment. Store the final benefit statement, appeal outcome and a copy of exactly what was submitted. Apply the insurer’s retention period without keeping sensitive data in unnecessary shared locations.

**Insurance and privacy disclaimer:** Claim requirements, access rights, translation standards and deadlines vary by policy, insurer and jurisdiction. This guide is organizational information, not legal, insurance, tax, privacy or medical advice. Obtain the current claim instructions and use secure, consented channels.

## FAQ

### Are the hospital receipt and itemized bill the same document?

No. The official receipt records the formal charge/payment total; the itemized list explains services, medicines and consumables. Insurers often require both.

### Do all Chinese medical records need certified translation?

Not necessarily. Ask the insurer which documents and certification level it requires. Preserve every Chinese source and pair translations separately.

### What if the final pathology is not ready when I leave China?

Submit available completed records if permitted, list the pathology as pending, record the expected date and arrange secure later delivery and translation.

### Can the insurer request records directly from the hospital?

Potentially, with required contract evidence, identity and patient authorization. Confirm the hospital process and do not assume direct access eliminates your document deadlines.

### How should I handle a mismatch in my English name?

Do not edit the medical document. Obtain an identity-link statement or explanation tying passport name, hospital registration and policyholder to the same person.
